# Seattle Eastside Chinese dining guide — research methodology

**Canonical methodology. Last updated: 2026-07-20.**

## Research question

Where is Chinese and Taiwanese dining on the Seattle Eastside active now, what
is each place specifically useful for, and how do the region's corridors,
chains, independent restaurants, grocery-food counters, and suburban gaps fit
together?

The guide does not ask for a single “best Chinese restaurant.” The useful unit
is a venue in a format and daypart, a regional lane, a dish or technique, or a
cluster that changes what is practical for nearby residents.

## Geography

Core coverage is Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland, Issaquah, Sammamish, Factoria,
Bel-Red, and Overlake. City labels follow postal identity, but cluster labels
follow dining behavior; the NE 20th/148th corridor crosses the intuitive
Bellevue–Redmond boundary and should be researched as one ecosystem.

Seattle proper, Renton, Kent, Bothell, and Richmond BC can be comparison markets
without being silently added to this dataset.

## Editorial commitments

1. Separate operations, food, service, atmosphere, parking, queue, price, and
   safety evidence.
2. Prefer dish- and use-case-specific conclusions to universal rankings.
3. Treat regional labels as claims requiring menu, self-description, or expert
   support; never infer cuisine from an owner's perceived identity.
4. Compare chains and independents honestly. A globally consistent chain can be
   useful without proving local culinary leadership.
5. Preserve suburb-to-suburb asymmetry. Bellevue has far more evidence and
   regional density than Sammamish; the dataset should show that rather than
   pad thin areas.
6. Preserve address, hour, closure, and move conflicts instead of averaging them.
7. Keep Chinese-American neighborhood restaurants in scope when their use-case
   is locally important, while labeling the lane accurately.
8. Do not convert star averages into an ethnicity-based “authenticity” score.

## Source hierarchy

### First-party and transactional operations

Official restaurant sites, current Toast/Chowbus pages, mall directories,
reservation systems, and current ordering pages are preferred for location,
phone, branch identity, hours, menus, and reservation method. A live ordering
page is operational evidence, not independent proof of food quality.

### Local expert and editorial reporting

Seattle Times food reporting, KUOW, Seattle Met, Seattle Magazine, Eater Seattle,
and The Infatuation are valuable for dish detail, comparative context, openings,
and cluster formation. Two outlets are not independent when one merely repeats
the other's reporting.

### Chinese-language and community evidence

Fantuan, Chinese social channels, current restaurant posts, and substantive
Chinese-language community discussion are important discovery and calibration
layers. Public web auditability is weaker here than it was for the Richmond BC
guide; the first pass therefore does not pretend to have completed a Chinese-
language consensus sweep. Any signed-in observation must be summarized in the
record with its date and limitations.

### Local community discussion

Eastside, Bellevue, Redmond, Kirkland, Issaquah, and Sammamish community forums
help identify openings, moves, dishes, crowding, and neighborhood dependence.
Concrete repeat-use reports are stronger than generic claims. Votes and repeated
comments inside one thread are not independent sources.

### Maps and aggregators

Apple Maps, current delivery pages, Restaurantji, Roadtrippers, and broad
directories may corroborate operations and expose recent reviews. They are not
strong quality arbiters. Conflicting aggregator closure flags trigger a recheck.

### Safety

Public Health – Seattle & King County is the primary inspection authority. Its
rating reflects a trend over inspections: `Excellent`, `Good`, `Okay`, or
`Needs to improve`. Safety findings are never treated as culinary endorsements
or authenticity evidence. Search portal:
https://cd.kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dph/health-safety/food-safety/search-restaurant-safety-ratings

## Admission and confidence

A strong `hot`, `rising`, or `staple` recommendation normally requires current
operations, at least two independent use/quality signals, and a defensible dish,
technique, or use-case. Missing evidence produces `watch`, `lead`, or
`new-lead`, not invented certainty.

`status` describes the current scene role; `confidence` describes the strength
of evidence. A brand-new restaurant may be `rising` with medium confidence. A
long-running neighborhood anchor may be `staple` with medium confidence when
recent dish-level reporting is thin.

## Recency

- Recheck new openings, moves, and transactional pages within 30–60 days.
- Prefer food reporting from the last 12 months, especially for `hot` and
  `rising` calls.
- Older evidence may establish lineage but cannot establish current execution.
- Recheck hours and reservations 48–72 hours before a high-stakes visit.

## Branch handling

Supreme Dumplings, Dan Gui, Dough Zone, and other multi-location businesses have
branch-specific operations. A Bellevue quality review cannot automatically set
the status of a new Redmond branch. Records may mention other locations, but
hours, confidence, and `lastVerified` belong to the named branch.

## Known first-pass biases

- Bellevue has much denser recent editorial coverage than the other Eastside
  cities.
- English-language outlets overexpose polished chains and new openings.
- Searchable web evidence underrepresents Chinese-language social and closed
  community discussion.
- Full-service Sichuan/Hunan restaurants are easier to classify than bakeries,
  breakfast counters, Cantonese barbecue, and Chinese-American neighborhood use.
- T&T is both a cultural/retail hub and a prepared-food operation; treating it
  as either only a supermarket or only a restaurant loses important context.

These are research problems to surface, not gaps to fill with weaker claims.
