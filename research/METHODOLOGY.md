# Richmond BC Chinese dining guide — research methodology

**Canonical methodology. Last updated: 2026-07-19.**

This document governs research and editorial decisions for the public Richmond BC Chinese dining guide. `data/` is the publishing source of truth; files in `research/` explain how evidence is gathered and interpreted.

The public guide is not a family itinerary. Personal trip constraints may be used privately to test whether the guide supports real decisions, but names, tickets, hotel details, and a family-specific schedule do not belong in public venue or hub records.

## 1. Research question

The guide asks where Richmond's Chinese dining scene is active **now**, what each place is specifically good for, how the geography and dayparts are changing, and how a diner can use that knowledge without mistaking popularity, décor, or one platform's star average for food quality.

It is not a universal ranking of “the best Chinese restaurants.” The useful unit is usually one of these:

- a venue in a particular format and daypart;
- a hub or cluster at a particular time;
- a restaurant's specific regional lane;
- a dish or technique, such as soy chicken, roast duck, dim-sum wrappers, or Hunan fresh-chile cooking.

This is why the guide can conclude that different siu-mei shops lead for different meats, or that a quiet mall at 8:00pm can still contain a very active lunch ecosystem.

## 2. Editorial commitments

1. Follow repeated local behaviour, not influencer aesthetics.
2. Discover broadly in Chinese and English; verify operational facts separately.
3. Treat Chinese-language audiences as diverse, not as one authentic voting bloc.
4. Compare like with like: food-court stalls with stalls, banquet rooms with banquet rooms, and delivery behaviour with delivery behaviour.
5. Separate food and technique from service, queue, parking, atmosphere, hygiene, and value. All matter, but they answer different questions.
6. Rank dishes or use-cases when the evidence supports that more strongly than an overall restaurant rank.
7. Preserve source disagreements and uncertainty instead of averaging them away.
8. Name the exact venue, unit, cluster, dish, and daypart whenever possible.
9. Prefer a blank field and an explicit caution to an unsupported operational claim.
10. Keep every published factual claim traceable to a dated source.

## 3. Source model

No source is authoritative for every kind of claim. Sources are assigned roles.

### A. First-party and primary operational sources

Restaurant and mall websites, current ordering pages, reservation systems, business posts, phone confirmation, municipal business licences, and health-inspection records are preferred for:

- existence and current location;
- phone, website, reservation method, and unit number;
- opening hours and kitchen cutoffs;
- stated format, menu, and payment options;
- moves, closures, and temporary schedules.

First-party sources are not treated as independent proof of food quality. An official page can also be stale; visible update dates and conflicts with current transactions must be recorded.

### B. Chinese transactional platforms

Fantuan review, delivery, pickup, deal-redemption, monthly-sales, and repeat-order pages are especially valuable because they expose behaviour, not just commentary. They are used for:

- current commercial activity;
- dishes people repeatedly buy;
- taste versus environment/service score splits;
- price and portion patterns;
- Chinese-language complaints and repeat-purchase signals.

As of 2026-07-19, Fantuan's public web restaurant pages expose a useful auditable subset without login: address, current-day hours, review count, taste/environment/service splits, selected dated reviews, note counts, deal-redemption counts, and named dishes. The app still contains more of the corpus. Cite the stable store-detail URL and summarize the exact web-visible observation; do not imply that a future reader can see every app review.

Limitations: delivery favours dishes that travel, platform promotions alter demand, ghost kitchens can appear beside dining rooms, and a live menu does not prove a walk-in location or every listed hour. Deal sales show commercial activity, not independent quality. Transaction data is compared within the platform and never converted into a universal score.

### C. Detailed Chinese-language local reports

RedNote/Xiaohongshu, Chinese local forums, Chinese-language community media, accessible public WeChat articles, and substantive Chinese-language reviews are used for:

- openings, moves, chef changes, and emerging clusters;
- regional expectations and dish-level comparisons;
- repeat-visit accounts;
- Chinese names, menu vocabulary, and ordering patterns;
- disagreements that English review sites may miss.

Limitations: RedNote overrepresents younger, mobile, often consumption-forward users and contains sponsored or engagement-optimized content. A polished post is a lead, not proof. Useful reports name dishes, dates, repeat visits, waits, prices, or changes from a prior visit. Follower count and visual production value do not increase evidentiary weight.

### D. Publicly auditable Chinese community and media sources

Sing Tao's current Vancouver food reporting, Westca/大温餐馆点评, archival OpenRice Canada pages, the closed Ming Pao Canada archive, and other publicly browsable Chinese-language sources can provide a re-checkable layer between signed-in social platforms and English-language directories.

Use them for:

- Cantonese/Hong Kong community reputation and dish vocabulary;
- dated restaurant profiles and chef interviews;
- openings, moves, closures, and historical continuity;
- corroborating claims that originated on RedNote, Fantuan, or a private forum.

OpenRice requires a Canada-specific freshness check before it is treated as current evidence. In July 2026, the current OpenRice region selector listed Hong Kong, Macau, Greater Bay Area, Taiwan, Japan, Thailand, Singapore, and the Philippines, but not Canada. Publicly indexed Canada/Vancouver results resolve to the separate `OpenRice Travel` archive and expose reviews from roughly 2009–2010. Those pages are useful historical evidence for Cantonese/Hong Kong dining lineage, but not a live Richmond consensus source.

Westca is public, searchable Chinese text with Richmond and cuisine filters plus dated detail-page reviews. It is especially useful for older-community vocabulary, long-lived restaurants, and historical reputation. Its volume is small, listings include closed venues, and many reviews are old, so it cannot establish current quality or operations without corroboration.

Sing Tao remains an active online Vancouver source in 2026 and is particularly useful for openings, closures, moves, ownership history, and older Cantonese-community continuity. It often reports from a restaurant's social announcement, so the underlying announcement and the article count as one operational signal, not two independent sources. Ming Pao Canada's Vancouver operation stopped publishing and updating in January 2026; its archive is historical only.

Chinese newspaper and community-media coverage is also not sponsorship-free. Distinguish reported observation from advertorial copy and restaurant-provided claims.

### E. Local community discussion

The Richmond/Vancouver subreddits, X threads, long-running forums, and neighbourhood discussions are useful for:

- current reputation shifts;
- “resting on reputation” versus rising-challenger hypotheses;
- queues, parking, dayparts, and closures;
- comparisons among places serving the same use-case.

Votes and repetition within one thread are not independent signals. Concrete dish comparisons and evidence of repeated personal use carry more weight than generic superlatives.

### F. Expert and editorial sources

Chinese Restaurant Awards, local restaurant reporting, and specialist food writers are used for:

- technique and chef leads;
- historical context and ownership succession;
- reported openings, moves, or closures;
- signature-dish hypotheses.

Awards tend to favour ambitious full-service restaurants and should not be used as a census of food courts or inexpensive everyday dining. A news article repeating an owner's RedNote post is the same underlying source for independence purposes.

### G. Maps and broad directories

Google Maps, Apple Maps, Restaurantji, Yellow Pages, Wanderlog, Waze, mall directories, and similar sources are useful for:

- addresses, phones, coordinates, and map identity;
- recent photos and operational-change clues;
- popular-times patterns;
- hours corroboration when first-party information is absent.

They are weak quality arbiters. Directory summaries may be generated, outdated, or internally inconsistent. Google is never the sole reason a place is called hot, rising, or fading.

### H. Food-safety and inspection records

Richmond is covered by **Vancouver Coastal Health (VCH), not Fraser Health**. Use VCH's restaurant-inspection and closure records as the primary safety source. Public mirrors such as Pass or Fail may improve searchability but should identify VCH as the underlying authority.

Inspection evidence is coded separately from taste, authenticity, popularity, and décor:

- record the inspection date, result, cited infraction, closure, and reopening when material;
- do not equate one routine infraction with a generally unsafe restaurant;
- do not treat “no infractions” as a quality endorsement;
- do not amplify an allegation from a forum when an official inspection record can resolve it;
- time-limit hygiene cautions and record corrective action or reopening.

Primary portal: https://travelclinic.vch.ca/en/health-topics/health-protection-permits-and-licensing

## 4. Cultural and platform calibration

The project does not apply a numerical “Asian reviewers grade harder” correction. That intuition identifies a real comparability problem, but no single ethnicity-based adjustment is defensible.

Instead:

- compare ratings only within similar platform, format, price, cuisine, and review-volume contexts;
- read why people scored a place low or high;
- distinguish taste from service/environment sub-scores when a platform exposes them;
- note review incentives and implausibly high acquisition patterns;
- value repeat visits, named dishes, regional benchmarks, and current transactions above generic praise;
- never infer a reviewer's ethnicity from a name, photograph, or writing style;
- use language and substantive culinary expectations as evidence, not identity assumptions.

A Fantuan listing with strong taste and weak service/environment can be a food-first recommendation. A polished restaurant with high service scores can also be excellent, but those scores do not prove regional cooking. A low Google score at an old, busy Cantonese restaurant and a near-perfect score at a recently promoted concept raise different questions; neither receives an automatic correction.

## 5. Evidence coding

For every serious candidate, code claims into separate dimensions:

- **Food/technique:** seasoning, texture, freshness, wok work, wrappers, roasting, stock, ingredient quality.
- **Dish fit:** what to order, what sells repeatedly, and whether one specialty is stronger than the rest.
- **Regional specificity:** how the menu, language, and cooking map to a supported regional tradition.
- **Consistency:** repeat visits, changes over time, chef or ownership continuity.
- **Value:** price, portion, bundles, and whether cost complaints concern absolute price or value received.
- **Operations:** location, hours, reservations, kitchen cutoff, payment, menu availability.
- **Friction:** queue, service, parking, noise, accessibility, and group fit.
- **Safety/hygiene:** inspection evidence and specific recent incidents, kept distinct from aesthetics.
- **Hype risk:** sponsorship, soft-opening enthusiasm, review incentives, or engagement-first presentation.

Do not turn these dimensions into one opaque weighted score. The published `status`, `confidence`, dishes, cautions, and evidence body should make the reasoning legible.

## 6. Independence, recency, and admission

### Independent signals

Two pages are not independent when one copies the other, both syndicate the same article, both quote the same owner's post, or one is an auto-generated summary of the other's reviews. Independence means separate underlying observation or transaction evidence.

### Recency rules

- Verify operational fields as close as practical to the publication date; for unstable or newly moved businesses, prefer evidence from the last 30–60 days.
- Prioritize quality reports from the last 12 months, with extra weight on the last 90 days for openings, moves, chef changes, and “rising/fading” claims.
- Older evidence may establish lineage or a long-running specialty but cannot alone establish current quality.
- A venue move requires the new address from a primary/current source. Stale delivery or review pages remain historical evidence only and must be labelled.
- Before a high-stakes visit, recheck hours and reservations 48–72 hours ahead. The public guide's `lastVerified` date is not a guarantee of holiday service.

### Admission rule

A strong `hot`, `rising`, or `staple` recommendation normally requires:

1. current operational verification;
2. at least two independent quality/use signals;
3. at least one detailed local, Chinese-language, expert, or transactional signal;
4. a specific dish, technique, or use-case that justifies inclusion.

When these conditions are incomplete, use `watch`, `lead`, or `new-lead`, lower confidence, blank disputed fields, and explicit cautions. Regional diversity is a research priority, not permission to lower the evidence bar silently.

## 7. Status and confidence are different

`status` is the editorial read on the venue's current place in the scene:

- `hot`: strong current demand and current evidence; may be established or new.
- `rising`: evidence is improving or a newer contender is gaining durable local use.
- `staple`: established, still useful, and currently operating; not necessarily fashionable.
- `watch`: meaningful reason to track, but material execution, operations, or evidence concerns remain.
- `fading`: credible evidence of declining relevance, quality, activity, or viability.
- `lead`: a candidate needing basic verification.
- `new-lead`: an opening/move/new concept not yet sufficiently verified.

`confidence` describes how strongly the available evidence supports the record:

- `high`: identity and operations are current; multiple independent signals support the editorial read.
- `medium`: the core read is supported, but an important field, disagreement, or source limitation remains.
- `low`: evidence is thin, old, or substantially conflicting.
- `lead`: not yet enough evidence for a settled judgment.

A venue can be `hot` with medium confidence after a move, or a `staple` with high confidence. Status must not be inferred from confidence.

## 8. Regional classification

Regional labels are supported by the restaurant's self-description, Chinese name, menu corpus, chef/ownership reporting, and dish patterns. Do not infer a cuisine from an owner's perceived ethnicity.

Use the narrowest defensible label, while preserving hybrids honestly. Examples include Cantonese/Hong Kong, Chaoshan, Hakka, Minnan/Taiwanese, Shanghai/Jiangnan, Shaanxi/Xi'an, Sichuan/Chengdu, Hunan, Yunnan/Southwest, Dongbei, and Xinjiang/Northwestern Chinese.

One dish does not necessarily define the whole restaurant. When platforms disagree—as with a restaurant classified as Shanghai but carrying significant Hunan dishes—record the mixed lane and explain it in the evidence body.

## 9. Hubs, dayparts, and off-peak claims

Hub health is not inferred from corridor appearance alone. Research each cluster by breakfast, lunch, early dinner, dinner, and late-night activity.

Popular-times data is a clue, not proof of food quality or stall-level crowding. An off-peak recommendation must check:

- whether the full menu is available;
- whether roast meats or other limited items have sold down;
- whether the kitchen is between services;
- whether the quiet period reflects a genuinely easier meal or a closing operation;
- whether the venue, not merely the mall, is open.

Coordinates for mall tenants may use a documented building centroid when a unit-level pin cannot be verified. The evidence body must say so.

## 10. Research workflow

1. **Discover:** collect named venues, dishes, moves, closures, and cluster claims across Chinese and local sources.
2. **Resolve identity:** match English and Chinese names, address, unit, aliases, and whether a page describes an old location.
3. **Verify operations:** confirm current address, phone, hours, website, reservations, payment, and format using the best available operational sources.
4. **Triangulate quality:** seek independent dish-level, repeat-visit, expert, and transaction evidence; actively search for disagreement.
5. **Classify:** assign region, format, status, confidence, daypart, dishes, and cautions using the rules above.
6. **Write:** structured facts go in YAML frontmatter; reasoning, Chinese-source notes, source conflicts, and limitations go in the Markdown body.
7. **Validate:** parse all YAML, ensure `id == filename`, verify required fields and hub IDs, require URL and date on each source, and do not hand-edit compiled JSON.
8. **Recheck:** revisit volatile records, especially moves, new openings, disputed hours, and `hot/rising/fading` claims.

## 11. Data and citation rules

- `data/SCHEMA.md` is the field contract.
- `data/venues/*.md` and `data/hubs/*.md` are the public guide's source records.
- Every source entry needs a descriptive label, direct URL, and observation date.
- The source label should say what the source supports; “website” is less useful than “official Richmond address and hours.”
- Strong claims in the body should be attributable to a listed source.
- Source disagreement belongs in both `cautions` when user-facing and the body when explanation is needed.
- Do not copy long review passages. Summarize, preserve meaning, and link.
- `site/content/restaurants.json` is generated output and is never hand-edited by research agents.

Unknown values stay `""`, `null`, or omitted. Blank data accompanied by a caution is better than false completeness.

## 12. Known coverage biases and active countermeasures

- **Legacy Cantonese bias:** Richmond's history and English coverage make Cantonese institutions easy to find. Counter by deliberate regional sweeps and menu-level classification.
- **Newcomer/social bias:** RedNote and delivery platforms can over-amplify newer mainland-oriented businesses. Counter with repeat transactions, local forums, older-community sources, and time.
- **Full-service award bias:** expert awards undercount food courts and everyday stalls. Maintain a separate stall/hub research lane.
- **Delivery bias:** travelling dishes and promotions dominate transaction data. Verify dine-in specialties separately.
- **Language bias:** Chinese-language evidence is essential but not automatically more truthful. Compare communities, platforms, formats, and generations.
- **Survivorship/reputation bias:** a long history can conceal decline, while a new opening can receive inflated soft-launch enthusiasm. Search explicitly for changes over time.
- **Accessibility bias:** private WeChat groups and video-first platforms are hard to archive. Record what was actually inspected and do not imply comprehensive access.
- **Auditability bias:** rich signed-in sources can dominate because they contain more detail, even when a future reviewer cannot reopen them. Summarize the observed evidence in the venue body and seek a public corroborating source such as Fantuan's web-visible detail page, Chinese community media, an official page, or a public directory. OpenRice Canada is archival unless a current Canada region reappears.
- **Operational-completeness bias:** phone, coordinates, or online booking may genuinely be unavailable for a stall. Do not downgrade its food solely for weak web infrastructure.

## 13. Update cadence

For a routine guide refresh:

1. Check all `hot`, `rising`, `watch`, `lead`, and `new-lead` records first.
2. Reconcile moves, closures, hours, and websites.
3. Refresh Fantuan transaction/dish signals and recent Chinese-language reports.
4. Audit one regional lane and one hub deeply rather than shallowly touching everything.
5. Recheck established `staple` records at least seasonally or before promoting them in a current guide.
6. Update `lastVerified` only when the record was actually rechecked; do not bulk-bump dates mechanically.
7. Record material changes and preserved disagreements in the body.

## 14. Current dataset and limitations

As of 2026-07-19, the dataset contains 31 venue records and 6 hubs. All venues have an address; most have current phone and weekly hours; fields that could not be reconciled remain blank. The current expansion added dish-specific siu-mei coverage, a second Sichuan and Shanghai/Jiangnan anchor, Hui/Ningxia halal cooking, late-night Cantonese `da laang`, and Hunan, Xinjiang, Dongbei, and Yunnan lanes.

Important limitations for the next pass:

- unit-level coordinates remain incomplete for many standalone venues;
- several stalls lack a verifiable public phone or full weekly schedule;
- dim-sum quality evidence is operationally strong but still uneven at the dish/technique level;
- dedicated Chaoshan and Hakka cooking, Shandong/Beijing, Uyghur-run dining, dessert, bakery, and late-night noodle coverage need an explicit gap audit; Hui/Ningxia halal cooking is now represented separately from Xinjiang;
- the dataset is a curated guide, not a census of every Richmond Chinese restaurant;
- signed-in and video-first sources are not always stable or fully archivable.
- publicly indexed OpenRice Canada pages are archival rather than an active 2026 Vancouver review corpus; use them for history, not current rankings.

## 15. Reviewer checklist

An independent reviewer should test the following rather than merely checking prose quality:

1. Are sources being used for claims they can actually support?
2. Are purportedly independent signals truly independent?
3. Are status and confidence calibrated consistently across old Cantonese institutions and newer mainland-oriented venues?
4. Do Chinese-platform signals receive appropriate scepticism about sponsorship, demographics, delivery bias, and promotions?
5. Are negative service/environment reports being separated fairly from food evidence without being minimized?
6. Are regional labels specific, defensible, and free of identity inference?
7. Are dish-level claims stronger than whole-restaurant claims where appropriate?
8. Are source disagreements and stale addresses/hours clearly visible to readers?
9. Does the curated set have meaningful blind spots by region, price, format, neighbourhood, or generation?
10. Do any records overclaim `hot`, `rising`, `staple`, or high confidence?
11. Are blanks honest, or is crucial verification simply unfinished?
12. Could another agent reproduce or update the work from the files alone?

Review findings should cite specific venue files and distinguish factual errors, methodological risks, and optional editorial improvements.
