# Empire Centre calibration case

> **Status:** Historical calibration case from the first research pass. It documents why raw ratings and mall appearance are misleading; current policy is in `research/METHODOLOGY.md` and current venue facts are in `data/`.

Observed July 19, 2026. This is a first-pass research note, not a recommendation list.

## Why Empire is useful

Empire demonstrates why raw Google ratings do not answer the project's question. Google mixes mall-level judgments with individual business judgments, and reviews often blend food with parking, seating, service, atmosphere, and the condition of the building.

## Current Google signals

- Empire Centre mall: 3.7 from 769 reviews.
- The directory currently labels 41 businesses as Food & Drink, though this includes full-service restaurants, food-court stalls, cafes, and at least one grocery store.
- Sunday's popular-times curve is strongly lunch-weighted: approximately 80% at 11 a.m., 94% at noon, 97–99% around 1–2 p.m., 81% at 4 p.m., 71% at 6 p.m., and 60% at 7 p.m.; the mall lists an 8 p.m. close.
- A recent mall review complains about too little seating because so many people come to dine. This contradicts the simple reading that a 3.7 mall is an unpopular food destination.

The spread is too wide and the review ecosystems too different to apply a single correction such as “add 0.4 for Chinese restaurants.” Double Double's 3.6, for example, must be read as a long-running inexpensive restaurant with 1,580 reviews, while a 4.9 hot-pot listing with a similar review count raises a different question about review acquisition and clientele.

## Off-peak hypotheses to verify

- Around 9:30–10:30 a.m. could work for Hong Kong breakfast before the lunch climb, but Cha Don and One Cafe may already draw lines and some dishes may be daypart-specific.
- Around 3–4 p.m. may offer easier seating after the lunch peak, but individual stalls can close early, take breaks, sell out, or reduce their menu.
- After 6 p.m. is not a general food-court strategy because key stalls close between 6 and 7 p.m.; late dining belongs to different full-service tenants or other Richmond clusters.

These windows require stall-level verification. Mall popular-times data is only a clue.

## Current external leads

- A March 18, 2026 *Vancouver Sun* Eat Streets report, syndicated by Yahoo Canada, describes weekday and especially weekend morning crowds at Cha Don and One Cafe. It also reports that The North Bistro / KSquared Mansion, formerly a northeastern-Chinese buffet, closed at the end of 2025.
- A May 19, 2026 Noms report says a Xi'an roujiamo stall called Meeting in Chang'an / 西安肉夹馍 was being built in a converted Empire unit. The report traces the information to the owner's RED post and says the project was waiting for inspection. Opening status is not yet verified.
- Tourism Richmond's March 14, 2025 Empire guide identifies One Cafe as Hong Kong-style, Cloud 9 as Taiwanese-style, and Cha Don as a Hong Kong breakfast destination. It is useful background but needs 2026 re-verification because tenant turnover is fast.

## Source-role decision

- Google: existence, address, suite, hours leads, recent photos, popular times, and operational complaints.
- Xiaohongshu/RED: current openings, dish-level discovery, Chinese-language local experience, and migration-driven trends.
- Dianping/大众点评: use when Richmond listing coverage and recency are adequate; compare only within the platform and inspect written content.
- Chinese Restaurant Awards: technique and signature-dish leads, especially for full-service restaurants; not a food-court census.
- Recent local reporting and forums: corroborate tenant changes, queues, and repeat local favorites.
