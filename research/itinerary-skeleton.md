### Off-peak late lunch

- Target Richmond arrival: approximately 3:15–4:00 p.m.; refine after routing.
- Primary hypothesis: a targeted Empire Centre food-court circuit. This is after the mall's lunch peak but before key stalls close at 6–7 p.m.
- The circuit should consist of two or three deliberately chosen dishes shared by the family, not random grazing at every stall.
- Required checks: Saturday kitchen breaks, sellouts, full-menu availability, parking, and exact closing time for each stall.
- Backup: a standalone restaurant that serves continuously through the afternoon, or a small Langley bridge snack followed by an earlier Richmond dinner if traffic collapses the window.

### Late dinner

- Target: approximately 8:30–9:30 p.m., giving at least four and preferably five hours after late lunch.
- Best geographic hypothesis: Alexandra Road or Capstan.
- Culinary role: contrast with lunch. If lunch is Cantonese/Hong Kong/Taiwanese food-court cooking, dinner should emphasize another region or format.
- Favor places whose kitchens remain fully active late, not restaurants technically open but already winding down.

## Sunday, August 2

### Farewell dim sum

- This is the weekend's main dim sum slot.
- Target either first seating (roughly 9:00–10:00 depending on restaurant) or a reserved late seating if departure time permits.
- Current strategic choices:
  - Empire Seafood at 9:00 for the easiest proven low-crowd option.
  - Continental at 10:00 for the distinctive cart experience, accepting more bustle.
  - A technique-focused challenger such as Jade, Golden Paramount, Lanxuan, or Yue if Chinese-source research demonstrates a clear advantage.
  - Fisherman's Terrace if the family wants a polished familiar benchmark.
- Optional final pickup: Parker Place BBQ or another roast-meat/bakery takeaway only if routing and departure time make it painless.

## Overall meal balance

The intended shape is:

1. Friday: full-service regional/late-night Richmond.
2. Saturday late lunch: food-court/stall exploration at an off-peak hour.
3. Saturday late dinner: a contrasting region or communal format.
4. Sunday: Cantonese dim sum tradition.

This provides four serious meals without making the weekend feel like continuous eating.
